# Learning Sequential Bell Limits

How can arbitrarily many observers each violate a Bell inequality, yet no protocol preserve a fixed violation through an arbitrarily long chain? This route connects the ordinary Bell experiment to the causal argument that answers that question.

The single educational anchor is **Valerio Scarani, _Bell Nonlocality_ (Oxford University Press, 2019)**. The [source bridge](scarani-bridge.md) identifies the relevant sections, translates notation, and supplies the steps specific to this project. Primary research papers remain in [related work](../references/related-work.md); they are attribution and proof dependencies, not additional educational anchors.

## The understanding map

The map contains exactly **30 milestones**: **10 background/preliminary milestones** and **20 narrative/innovation milestones**. The background assembles probability tables, Bell locality, measurement disturbance, temporal records and statistical evidence into one experimental model. The narrative moves from its one-step predictability mechanism to the full causal bound, attainable region, quantum rate and operational consequences. Each phase ends with a synthesis.

These are common understanding targets, not a learner's assessment or progress record. The map does not replace the detailed proofs or claim to cover every technical calculation. The scientific pages remain authoritative if an introductory explanation leaves a detail implicit.

## Three conclusions to keep separate

The nonnegative-gap region is **exact over time-ordered nonsignalling behaviors**. The quantum construction establishes the **optimal exponential rate**, not an exact finite-depth quantum optimum. The statistical theorem supplies a **necessary source count for iid marginal-data tests**, not a matching sample complexity or a limit on all uses of the complete transcript.
